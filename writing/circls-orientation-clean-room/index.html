Optimizing Surface-Code Patch Orientation in CircLS

Changing only the starting orientation of data patches reduced the modeled space-time cost of 10 out of 26 verified compilations, without increasing their latency.

Richard Williams August 31, 2026 Research report
31 cases selected before results were known
10 of 26 verified compilations used less occupied space over time
1.62% less occupied space-time across all verified cases, weighted by original cost
Unchanged modeled latency in all 10 improved compilations

CircLS is a compiler for modeled fault-tolerant quantum circuits. It represents each logical data qubit as a surface-code patch. A patch has two possible starting orientations: rotating it swaps which directions its X and Z boundaries face. The quantum program does not change, but the compiler may be able to place and connect the patches more efficiently.

CircLS calls this starting choice a patch's birth orientation. We tested whether searching those choices could improve the output of the released compiler. Every comparison starts with CircLS's own optimized full configuration. We then change orientations, rerun placement and the complete compilation pipeline, and accept a result only if it uses less occupied space over time, adds no latency, reproduces exactly, and passes every public verifier check.

This is a compiler optimization. It does not change a quantum algorithm, execute a quantum computer, or establish an advantage for Shor's algorithm.

What the measurements mean

The labels V1, T1, and V2 are names used by CircLS, not terms a quantum-computing reader should be expected to recognize without explanation.

  • Occupied space over time (V1) is the primary measurement. CircLS adds up the number of surface-code tiles in use during every round and divides by the code distance. A lower number means less total occupancy across the compiled circuit.
  • Modeled latency (T1) is the number of measurement layers. We rejected any candidate that increased it.
  • Physical qubit-rounds (V2) is a second measure of total resource use.
  • Reserved rectangle (V3) is the area of the smallest rectangle containing every tile the circuit touches, multiplied by the modeled duration and normalized by code distance. It can grow even when actual occupied space-time shrinks.

The exact definitions are in CircLS's metric code. These are estimates for compiler-generated circuits, not wall-clock runtime or measurements from quantum hardware.

Complete census

We selected the test set before running this experiment. It contained every available CircLS case from 2 through 32 logical qubits, except one TopoLS-derived case whose separate source checkout was unavailable. No case was added or removed because an earlier search looked promising.

Primary result

  • 31 cases were attempted. The released baseline passed every required verification check in 26 of them, so those 26 entered the performance comparison.
  • The one-patch-at-a-time search reduced occupied space-time in 10 of the 26 verified cases. It found no improvement in the other 16.
  • Across all 26 cases, including the zeros, the average reduction per case was 1.67%, the median was 0%, and the combined reduction weighted by each case's original cost was 1.62%.
  • All 10 improvements kept the same modeled latency as the released compiler.
  • Three improvements required a larger reserved rectangle, so lower occupied space-time does not automatically mean a smaller chip footprint.

The three reserved-rectangle regressions—twistedghz_8, teleport_8, and dj_32—matter. Those results use fewer resources across time, but their layouts spread across a larger rectangle. The preferred result therefore depends on whether the machine is constrained by total work, peak active patches, or fixed chip area.

Cases without a reported improvement

The one-patch search found no gain in deutsch_n2, grover_n2, iswap_n2, cat_state_n4, hs4_n4, lpn_n5, bv_8, dj_8, ghz_8, bbpssw_4, bv_16, ghz_16, bbpssw_8, cat_state_n22, ghz_state_n23, and ghz_32. That does not prove that no better combination exists; the exhaustive experiment below finds exactly that situation in two cases.

Five released baselines—qrng_n4, steane_encode, graphstate_8, graphstate_16, and graphstate_32—did not complete every required public verification check. They are reported among the 31 attempted cases but excluded from the 26-case performance comparison.

As a secondary, after-the-fact check, we looked at the 14 verified cases longer than two measurement layers. Ten improved. Their average reduction was 3.10%, their median was 1.01%, and their weighted combined reduction was 1.65%. Because this subgroup was defined after the primary census, it is suggestive rather than a separate confirmed result.

How the search worked

Each case began with CircLS commit 3523afeb0034bf047651a7e98a7e45d3da5df72d and its released full configuration. That baseline includes optimized assignment, measurement reduction, liveness-based allocation, scheduling, routing, physical Stim lowering, and the public verifier. It was not a deliberately weakened control.

  1. Compile the unmodified released baseline and require every public verification check to run and pass.
  2. Record the starting orientation that CircLS chose for every data patch.
  3. Turn one patch at a time. For each possibility, rerun optimized placement and the complete CircLS compilation.
  4. Keep a candidate only if occupied space-time is strictly lower and modeled latency is no higher.
  5. If several candidates qualify, rank them by occupied space-time, then latency, then patch name.
  6. Recompile the candidates independently. Accept the first only if its circuit hash and measurements match exactly and every public verification check passes.
  7. Start another round from the accepted result. Stop when turning any single patch fails to help.

The optimization term for this procedure is deterministic steepest coordinate descent. More simply, it repeatedly takes the best verified one-patch change. When it stops, we know that no single patch helps; we do not yet know whether changing two or more patches together would help.

Verification and run accounting

Every accepted change passed CircLS's public verifier, including checks for silence, deterministic observables, graphlike distance, and logical equivalence. The census performed 854 complete candidate compilations and 58 full verification calls. Nine candidate compilations failed, all while searching teleport_8; they were recorded as rejected attempts rather than omitted.

Testing every orientation on small cases

A one-patch-at-a-time search can miss a result that requires several coordinated changes. We tested this directly on all 14 census cases with at most eight data qubits. Eleven had verified baselines. For each of those 11, we compiled every possible combination of patch orientations.

This required 1,116 complete compilations, with no compilation failures. Every best improving result was independently recompiled and fully verified. “Best” here means the best patch-orientation combination under the fixed CircLS setup. It does not mean the best possible layout, schedule, route, or quantum circuit.

The one-patch search found the best possible orientation in 9 of the 11 verified small cases. It missed bv_8 and dj_8. In both, changing either patch by itself has no effect, but changing the pair reduces occupied space-time. Because the simple search refuses a first step that does not immediately help, it can never reach the better pair.

Using the already compiled results, we then simulated a search allowed to consider changes to two patches at once. The one-patch policy examined 96 distinct assignments and found the best result in 9 of 11 cases. The two-patch policy examined 295 assignments and found the best result in all 11, compared with 1,116 assignments for exhaustive enumeration. This is an offline analysis of small cases, not yet evidence that a two-patch search will scale efficiently.

Interpretation

Starting patch orientation materially affected 10 of the 26 verified circuits in this test set. The extra search produced less occupied space-time in those cases without adding measurement layers. The effect was not universal, and the 1.62% combined reduction across the full verified set is modest.

The exhaustive experiment identifies a more specific opportunity: one-at-a-time search can fail even on small circuits because two changes may need to be made together. Allowing pairs repaired both observed failures and still examined far fewer assignments than exhaustive search.

This is stronger evidence than a collection of successful examples: the test set was fixed first, unsuccessful and excluded cases are reported, tradeoffs in other measurements are visible, and the small results are compared with the exact best orientation. It is still better described as a focused compiler study and a research lead than as a complete standalone paper contribution.

The closest prior work is O3LS, which already studies orientation-aware automatic layout search for lattice surgery. Harvest jointly optimizes placement, routing, scheduling, and resource supply. Our method is currently a simple, expensive search wrapped around CircLS's existing orientation input. We have not yet completed a direct comparison with a runnable O3LS optimizer.

Can a scalable search capture the benefit of changing two patches together without recompiling every possible pair on larger circuits?

A stronger follow-up should define that search before testing it, compare it with released CircLS, one-patch search, and random orientation sampling, and continue using exhaustive results wherever they remain affordable. It should also use a separately frozen random-circuit test set, extend to larger circuits, and compare with an actual O3LS implementation or a carefully matched reimplementation.

Reproduction record

The public inputs are the pinned CircLS checkout, CircLS paper, pinned QASMBench checkout, the complete method above, and the machine-readable corpus manifest. The run used macOS 26.6.2 on arm64, Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1, NetworkX 3.6.1, Stim/Sinter 1.16.0, PyMatching 2.4.0, and NWQEC 0.1.2.

An independent reproducer should implement the method from the public description, freeze source revisions, dependencies, platform and BLAS/LAPACK identity, generated-QASM hashes, code, commands, and outputs before comparing detailed endpoints. Report exact endpoint agreement, metric agreement with a different endpoint, compile failure, and verification failure separately. A frozen placement replay is not a search reproduction.

Claim boundaries

  • No change to the mathematical quantum algorithms and no Shor result.
  • No quantum-hardware experiment, hardware runtime, fidelity, or logical-error-rate result.
  • No non-Clifford, T-gate, or magic-state-factory evaluation at this CircLS pin.
  • No claim that lower occupied space-time alone determines the preferred hardware layout; three improvements enlarge the reserved rectangle.
  • No global-optimality claim beyond birth orientation for the 11 exhaustively enumerated small cases.
  • No independent external reproduction or new cross-platform replay of the complete census yet.
  • No established novelty over O3LS or Harvest.
