Birth-Orientation Optimization in CircLS

A complete benchmark census of a verifier-gated orientation search, followed by exhaustive analysis of every eligible case with at most eight data qubits.

Richard Williams August 31, 2026 Research report
31 cases frozen before the census
10 / 26 eligible cases with lower V1
1.62% aggregate V1-weighted reduction
9 / 11 small cases where strict descent reached the global orientation optimum

CircLS maps logical Clifford circuits to modeled lattice-surgery operations. Each data patch can be born in either of two orientations. That choice leaves the logical computation unchanged, but it changes which X and Z boundaries face each direction and can therefore change placement, routing, and the occupied spacetime of the compiled circuit.

This study adds an outer search over that existing compiler input. It does not improve a quantum algorithm, execute a quantum computer, or estimate an advantage for Shor's algorithm. The result is narrower: for this pinned CircLS backend and benchmark roster, changing patch birth orientations sometimes produces a smaller verified physical compilation than CircLS's released full configuration.

Complete census

The primary roster was frozen before the run and contained every available CircLS case from 2 through 32 logical qubits, except one TopoLS-derived case whose separate source checkout was unavailable. No case was selected because an earlier search had found a gain.

Primary result

  • 31 cases were attempted; 26 had released baselines that passed every required public verification check.
  • 10 of 26 eligible cases improved and 16 had zero gain under strict one-flip coordinate descent.
  • Mean V1 reduction, including zeros, was 1.67%; median reduction was 0%; aggregate V1-weighted reduction was 1.62%.
  • Every accepted endpoint retained the baseline T1 latency.
  • Three V1 winners enlarged the rectangular bounding box, so the result is not an unqualified area reduction.

Zero-gain and ineligible cases

The 16 fully verified zero-gain cases were deutsch_n2, grover_n2, iswap_n2, cat_state_n4, hs4_n4, lpn_n5, bv_8, dj_8, ghz_8, bbpssw_4, bv_16, ghz_16, bbpssw_8, cat_state_n22, ghz_state_n23, and ghz_32. “Zero” means that the declared one-flip search accepted no improvement; it does not prove that no better multi-flip orientation exists.

The five ineligible baselines were qrng_n4, steane_encode, graphstate_8, graphstate_16, and graphstate_32. They remain in the denominator of attempted cases but not in the verified performance comparison because their released baselines did not complete every forced public verifier check.

A post-census look at the 14 eligible cases with T1 greater than two found 10 improvements, a 3.10% mean reduction, a 1.01% median, and a 1.65% aggregate V1-weighted reduction. This subgroup is exploratory; it was not a separately predeclared primary endpoint.

What was measured

At the pinned code distance d = 3, CircLS defines V1 as the sum of occupied tiles in each round divided by three. T1 is the number of measurement layers. V2 is the physical qubit-round count. Peak allocated tiles and rectangular bounding-box volume provide two additional views of spatial cost. The implementation is public in CircLS's metric code.

These are resource measures of a compiler-generated, modeled fault-tolerant circuit. V1 is not wall-clock runtime. A lower V1 does not by itself demonstrate lower hardware error, less reserved chip area, or a faster quantum computer.

Frozen method

Each case began with CircLS commit 3523afeb0034bf047651a7e98a7e45d3da5df72d and its released full configuration. That baseline includes optimized assignment, measurement reduction, liveness-based allocation, scheduling, routing, physical Stim lowering, and the public verifier. It was not a deliberately weakened control.

  1. Compile the unmodified released baseline and require every public verification check to run and pass, forcing the graphlike-distance check above its default detector cap.
  2. Use the realized orientation of every data patch as the current state.
  3. Flip each data-patch orientation once, independently. For every candidate, rerun optimized placement and the complete physical CircLS compilation.
  4. Keep a candidate only if exact V1 is lower and exact T1 is no larger than the current state.
  5. Rank eligible candidates by V1, then T1, then coordinate name.
  6. Independently recompile candidates in that order. Accept the first only when its circuit hash and metrics reproduce exactly and every public verification check passes.
  7. Repeat until a complete one-flip neighborhood contains no accepted improvement.

This is deterministic steepest coordinate descent from the released baseline. It establishes a one-flip local optimum, not a global optimum. No route proxy was used in the census.

Verification and run accounting

Every accepted move and final endpoint passed CircLS's public verifier: silence, deterministic observables, forced graphlike distance, and logical affine equivalence. The census performed 854 complete candidate compilations and 58 full verification calls over 3,862 aggregate case-seconds. Nine candidate compilations failed, all while screening teleport_8; they were recorded as rejected candidates rather than removed from the run.

Exact small-instance comparison

Coordinate descent can stop at a local optimum. To measure that failure directly, the follow-up experiment took all 14 census cases with at most eight data qubits. Eleven had fully verified baselines; three were reported as ineligible. Every one of the 2^n birth-orientation assignments was compiled through CircLS's modeled physical-circuit pipeline for each eligible case.

The comparator compiled 1,116 assignments with zero compilation failures. Every improving global endpoint was independently recompiled and fully verified. “Global” here means global only over birth orientation while retaining CircLS's placement optimizer and every other pinned compiler choice—not global over all layouts, schedules, routes, or circuits.

Strict coordinate descent reached the global V1 optimum in 9 of the 11 eligible cases. It missed bv_8 and dj_8: in each landscape, either constituent flip leaves V1 unchanged, but taking both lowers it. The strict rule rejects the neutral first step and cannot cross the plateau. This turns a general suspicion about greedy search into an observed, exactly enumerated failure mode.

An offline simulation over these already compiled landscapes compared Hamming-radius-one and radius-two neighborhoods. Radius one again reached 9 of 11 global optima after consulting 96 unique states. Radius two reached all 11 after consulting 295 unique states, versus 1,116 assignments for exhaustive enumeration. This is evidence about search policy on small frozen landscapes—not a new physical compilation run and not proof that radius-two search scales efficiently.

Interpretation

The full census supports a defensible statement: birth orientation is a consequential CircLS mapping input on a substantial minority of this verified 2–32-qubit roster, and a deterministic verifier-gated search can recover lower-V1 physical compilations without increasing T1. The exhaustive study adds a separate result: strict one-flip descent has a concrete plateau failure, and a two-coordinate neighborhood repairs it on every tractable case tested.

The work is materially stronger than a collection of selected positive examples because it establishes a frozen denominator, publishes zeros and exclusions, reports secondary-metric regressions, and compares the heuristic with exact optima where tractable. It is still better described as a focused compiler study and research lead than as a complete standalone paper contribution.

The closest novelty challenge is O3LS, which already studies orientation-sensitive automatic lattice-surgery layout search. Harvest already co-optimizes placement, routing, scheduling, and resource supply. Our current method is a simple and expensive CircLS-specific outer loop over an existing orientation hook. No comparison against a runnable O3LS optimizer has yet been completed.

The next research question is whether a deterministic joint-orientation search can retain the small-instance quality of radius-two descent without requiring a quadratic number of full physical compilations at larger scales.

A stronger follow-up should predeclare a plateau-aware or bounded beam-search algorithm; compare released CircLS, strict descent, random orientation sampling, and exhaustive optima where tractable; freeze a separate random-circuit panel; extend to larger cases; and compare against an actual O3LS implementation or a carefully matched reimplementation.

Reproduction record

The public inputs are the pinned CircLS checkout, CircLS paper, pinned QASMBench checkout, the complete method above, and the machine-readable corpus manifest. The run used macOS 26.6.2 on arm64, Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1, NetworkX 3.6.1, Stim/Sinter 1.16.0, PyMatching 2.4.0, and NWQEC 0.1.2.

An independent reproducer should implement the method from the public description, freeze source revisions, dependencies, platform and BLAS/LAPACK identity, generated-QASM hashes, code, commands, and outputs before comparing detailed endpoints. Report exact endpoint agreement, metric agreement with a different endpoint, compile failure, and verification failure separately. A frozen placement replay is not a search reproduction.

Claim boundaries

  • No change to the mathematical quantum algorithms and no Shor result.
  • No quantum-hardware experiment, hardware runtime, fidelity, or logical-error-rate result.
  • No non-Clifford, T-gate, or magic-state-factory evaluation at this CircLS pin.
  • No claim that V1 alone determines the preferred hardware layout; three V1 improvements enlarge the bounding box.
  • No global-optimality claim beyond birth orientation for the 11 exhaustively enumerated small cases.
  • No independent external reproduction or new cross-platform replay of the complete census yet.
  • No established novelty over O3LS or Harvest.
